# BirdJournal — design decisions (grill sessions, 2026-09-26)

Source of truth for choices made on top of `spec.md`. Where this file and the spec disagree, this file wins.

## Product
- Glasses-first. The lens is the primary UI; the phone gets a minimal screen in phase D and social features later (out of scope). _Social features are now in scope: see the v2 spec, `docs/spec-v2-collection-and-circles.md` (grill session 2026-10-13, issue #48), and ADR-0001 to ADR-0006._
- Personal project, published personally. Only the latest iOS is supported: deployment target iOS 27, Swift 6 language mode, Xcode 27.
- Android: out of scope. Web-app path: dropped (Web Apps have no mic or camera).

## Toolkit facts that shape the design
- Ambient audio only arrives in-band on a camera stream (`StreamConfiguration(audioCodec: .pcm(...))`). HFP is 8 kHz and beamformed to the wearer's voice; not usable.
- Camera audio, Inputs, photo capture, motion, speech and voice invocations are experimental in SDK 1.0.0: Dev Mode and Beta channel only.
- Real glasses do not deliver the Back event to apps (DAT 1.0.0 Inputs docs, "Current limitations": the on-glasses path is not wired yet; `consumeBack` only matters on the mock). The Neural Band's middle-finger tap is the system's back gesture, and since every view an app sends is the root as far as the glasses know, it ends the display session from any card (seen on hardware, #24). The app must give its own way back: swipe right on a card returns to the list. The two-finger temple tap also ends the session.
- An app that subscribes to Inputs receives Nav and Select itself; the glasses neither moved focus between tappable rows on the species list nor scrolled a tall card (#24, on hardware; the Inputs docs: subscribing "avoids consuming interactions that the system or on-glasses display would otherwise handle"). So a list is a menu the app drives: it keeps the selection, highlights it and re-sends; and long content is paged by the app. A Display button click still arrives (the details page's "Add to my list").
- Display: one root `FlexBox` per `send`, 600×600 additive display, dims and sleeps on inactivity. Images from bundled `UIImage` work offline. The docs say a tall view scrolls vertically, but with Inputs attached the glasses hand swipe up/down to the app and do not scroll (2026-09-27, #24), and the mock preview anchors overflow at the bottom. So every send fits the canvas and the app pages: a species has a photo page and a details page.
- A bundled `UIImage` is laid out at its pixel size on the 600-pixel canvas (measured on the mock in #7, to verify on hardware): a 600 px crop fills the card and pushes the text off it. Lens crops are 552 × 368 (3:2): the card's width under 24 px of padding, short enough that the name stays on the first screenful (#24; the 260 px square of #7 sat beside the name and left half the canvas blank).
- Only one third-party app can be registered in Dev Mode at a time. Bundle IDs may not contain `-`.
- One device session per device (`DeviceSessionError.sessionAlreadyExists`). The listening run (#9) starts one session and shares it between the camera stream, Display and Inputs; the single-capability screens and mock tests still start their own.
- Display 1.0.0 has no wake call. The docs say the display dims after 20 s and sleeps at 25 s without ending the session; the app re-sends the current card (the species list or the species card with its refreshed status strip) on each new species, and whether that wakes a sleeping display is checked on hardware (#9, #24).
- MockDeviceKit cannot inject audio frames; sound-ID tests run on recorded files. With a still-image feed it delivers no video frames either (#9), so the frame path is tested with a stand-in source and the real stream's frames on hardware.

## Audio and identification
- Glasses stream: camera at `.low` resolution, lowest frame rate, audio PCM mono. Sample rate 44.1 kHz (chosen in the phase A spike), resampled to the model rate.
- Video frames are not used for identification. The most recent frame at the moment of the "Add to my list" tap (#24; it was the confirm tap in #9) is stored with the sighting as a JPEG under Application Support/Frames, referenced by a relative path; the camera-assist re-ranker is out of scope.
- Model: BirdNET+ V3.0 preview 3.1 (11,560 species, 32 kHz input, CC BY-SA 4.0 weights, "Powered by BirdNET" attribution). Pinned to the exact Zenodo file. Engine sits behind a protocol so v2.4 can be swapped in.
- Geo prior: BirdNET geomodel v3.0.4 (Apache-2.0), raw lat/lon/week input, used as a pre-filter with the reference threshold 0.03. No GBIF/H3 tables in v1.
- Runtime: ONNX Runtime via the official Swift package (`microsoft/onnxruntime-swift-package-manager`), consumed through the fork `lightsailvr/onnxruntime-swift-package-manager` so Xcode Cloud can fetch it. Core ML conversion is a later optimization, not a dependency.
- Audio source is a protocol with three implementations: glasses stream, phone mic, WAV file (tests).
- Windows: 3 s with 1.5 s overlap; a species enters the stack after two windows above threshold. Thresholds tuned in phase B.
- Ring buffer exists only for live playback. No audio is saved to the album.
- Location: "when in use" permission; Core Location live updates read for the whole run and held through the background by a `CLBackgroundActivitySession` (`UIBackgroundModes` has `location`), so every add records a fresh fix (#44, which replaced the one fix at start plus a ten-minute refresh).
- Background: the app must keep listening with the phone locked (audio + processing + Bluetooth background modes). Verified in phase A.

## Lens UI
- Gestures: swipe left/right = Nav, swipe up/down = Nav too (selection on the list, photo ⇄ details on a species; the glasses do not scroll for an app that subscribes to Inputs), tap = Select. Middle-finger tap and two-finger tap = quit (system; Back never reaches the app on real glasses).
- Page map (#24, replaces the #7 photo/description/confirm/saved pages; _2026-10-10 adds a sounds page above the photo and a viewfinder after "Add to my list", see "Sounds page and viewfinder"_): the root is the **species list** ("N species heard", one row per species heard, newest last: name, match rate, a photo icon when the pack has a card, a check when added), driven as a menu: swipe down/up move the highlighted row, a tap or swipe left opens it. The list is paged five rows at a time around the selection (a re-send starts at the top and the glasses do not scroll); the hint line says which page. Two pages per species: the **photo page** (the photo first and full width, the common name with the match rate beside it, the status strip "4 species · 2 of 4" with "· Added" once added, and the hint "Swipe down for more information · swipe right: all species", or with reference clips "Tap: its song · swipe down: more · right: all species"; a tap never adds a bird: it plays the clips, #41, below) and, on swipe down, the **details page** (name and match rate, field marks, size · habitat, the photo credit and the **"Add to my list"** primary button, or "Added to my list ✓"). Swipe up returns to the photo. On either page swipe left is the next species' photo and **swipe right is back to the list** (the only way back, since Back never reaches the app), with that species selected. Every page is one screenful inside the word budget.
- _Superseded 2026-10-10 by "Sounds page and viewfinder": "Add to my list" now opens a viewfinder, and the photo is taken there._ "Add to my list" saves in one action: the sighting is written with the camera frame from the moment of the tap. Both a Display button click and an Inputs Select on the details page save. A press delivered both ways (the open question from #7/#9) is two saves of one species within a second, which the adapter drops as an echo; a later tap on an added species' details page saves again and updates the sighting (spec user story 33). There is no confirm page. On the list, an Inputs Select opens the highlighted row.
- Stack never reorders; new species append at the end. A new species changes the status strip and the list but never moves the wearer off their card and never sends them to the root. Back from the list ends the session; Back from a card returns to the list.
- Photo-first cards: close-up crops with dark backgrounds preferred. Text ≤ ~40 words per **screenful** (the card's first screenful is strip, photo, name and match rate; the second is the text, credit and button); the list splits its rows into screenfuls the same way.
- Settled on hardware (#24): the glasses neither scroll a tall view nor move focus between tappable rows while the app subscribes to Inputs (swipes reach the app instead); the middle-finger tap ends the session (Back never reaches the app). Still to verify (DAT-SETUP-CHECKLIST.md): whether Select on the details page's button arrives as a click, an Inputs select or both (both add, deduplicated).
- Audio chirp on new candidate: off by default.
- Session start: phone button in v1; "Hey Meta, start BirdJournal" once the Developer Center approves Voice Invocation.

## Species pack
- Region v1: Los Angeles area, ~150 species with photos and descriptions. All BirdNET species remain identifiable by name.
- LA pack bundled in the app binary (offline on first launch). Other packs downloaded from GitHub Releases via a JSON index into app storage.
- Pack = zip of SQLite + pre-sized JPEGs (lens 552 × 368, see the Display image-size fact above; phone ~1200 px). A bird too tall for the card-shaped crop keeps its head and loses its tail.
- Pack builder (Python) adds a detector step (COCO "bird") to crop close-ups, scores crops on bird area, background luminance and sharpness, keeps top 3–5 per species, with a manual override list.
- Photo licenses: CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC. Every photo carries observer, license and source URL. Credit shown on the species card and in a full credits screen on the phone. _Since #50 (ADR-0001) photos are CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA only: see "Open-license pack photos"._
- Pack builder facts (#8): the iNaturalist API is the default metadata source (the Open Data dump is tens of GB; the DuckDB path over it exists for full rebuilds), ordered newest first because the most-faved observations are the oddities (every top-voted California Towhee was leucistic). Detector: COCO SSD MobileNet v1 (ONNX zoo, Apache-2.0); it misses birds under about 5 % of the frame, which are poor cards anyway. Crops below 0.4 sharpness are dropped before ranking; hand-held and dead birds still need the override file. Built packs are not committed: `packs/manifest.json` pins each to a zip on this repo's GitHub Releases and `scripts/download-pack.sh` fetches it (since #12; the ten-species pack was committed while it was 9 MB).
- Descriptions (#12) are cut from Wikipedia by rule, not written by hand: about 150 species is too many to author and keep reproducible, and the issue asked for Wikipedia-derived text. `overrides.json` replaces any field for a species the rules serve badly. Lookalikes stay empty in v1: no lens page shows them.

## Phase A go/no-go (issue #3) — GO, 2026-09-26
Run: glasses source, 44.1 kHz mono, `.low` at 2 fps, iPhone 17 Pro locked in a pocket, 19 min 45 s (`spike-20260926-193507.log`).
- Sample rate: **44.1 kHz**. 48 kHz is untested: the first attempt stalled on the Meta AI microphone grant, not on the rate.
- Coverage: 1,182.0 s of audio in 1,185.2 s of wall time (99.7 %; the shortfall is stream startup). Zero gaps over 2 s. Chunks every 23.2 ms on average (1,024 samples); the longest interval between chunks was 671 ms.
- Latency above best: 39–89 ms mean per 30 s interval, 689 ms worst. No upward drift over the run, so the link does not back up. Absolute glasses latency is unknown: presentation times are not on the phone's host clock (first offset 882,188 s).
- Glasses battery 100 → 88 % (about 0.6 %/min, so roughly 2.5–3 h of listening from full). Glasses thermal `none` throughout. Phone battery 65 → 65 %, thermal `nominal`.
- Background: status lines kept arriving every 30 s while locked (`background_locked` for 37 of 40 intervals). The Bluetooth and external-accessory modes are enough; no `AVAudioSession` is needed on the glasses path.
- One `stream_error "Critical error, the stream should end"` arrived 2 s after the app went to the background, but the stream kept `streaming` and audio never stopped. Watch for it; do not stop the source on stream errors alone.
- The first audio chunk after `streaming` was empty (0 samples); consumers must tolerate empty chunks.
- Doff does **not** pause (`spike-20260926-195716.log`): 12 s after taking the glasses off, the device ended the session (`session_error "Session ended by device"`, session and stream `stopped`) and the link dropped. After putting them back on, the glasses were `donned` and `connected` within about 35 s, but the session stayed stopped and no audio returned. The app must start a new session itself once the glasses are worn and connected again; waiting for `.paused` → `.started` never happens. Touchpad-tap pause is still untested.
- Verdict: **GO** for the audio path: continuous, survives a locked phone, and costs about 12 % glasses battery per 20 minutes. Follow-up needed: automatic session restart after doff/don (the listening session must survive taking the glasses off). Built in #10, see "Pause, disconnect and error handling".

## Los Angeles pack at 160 species (issue #12) — built 2026-09-27
- **The list**: the 150 bird species with the most research-grade iNaturalist observations in Los Angeles County (place 962, queried 2026-09-27), minus hybrids, the domestic Swan and Greylag Geese and the California Condor (no BirdNET class), plus ten species heard more than seen (Mountain Chickadee, Hutton's and Bell's Vireos, Cactus and Canyon Wrens, Common Poorwill, California Gnatcatcher, Greater Roadrunner, Western Screech Owl, Swainson's Thrush): 160 species, in observation order, in `packbuilder/packs/us-ca-la/pack.json`. Established exotics stay in (parrots, Red-whiskered Bulbul, Scaly-breasted Munia, Pin-tailed Whydah, Indian Peafowl, Egyptian Goose): they are what one hears in LA. Names are the BirdNET+ label file's (iNaturalist's Vireo swainsoni is BirdNET's Vireo gilvus; "Myrtle Warbler", "Rock Dove", "Scarlet Flycatcher" are the label file's spellings), so the engine, the lens and the album agree; `tests/test_definition.py` checks every entry against the label file.
- **Descriptions** come from each species' English Wikipedia article (the plain-text extract, cached by title): the summary is the lead's first sentences (60 words), the field marks are the Description section's plumage sentences with measurement sentences skipped (20 words, the details page's budget after name, size and habitat, credit and button), the size is the body length (a figure in a sentence that names the wing, tail, bill or egg counts only when its own clause says "long" or "length"; millimetres, metres and inches converted; 147 of 160 articles give one, the rest none rather than a wrong one), the habitat is the three habitat terms the article uses most from a fixed vocabulary. An article whose lead does not name the binomial (iNaturalist's "Bushtit" link lands on the family page) is refused and logged so the override file can fill it. The text is CC BY-SA 4.0: every species row records the article revision (`description_source`), the LICENSE lists them under "Text credits", the phone's species page and the credits screen link them. Pack schema 2.
- **Builder**: `large` renditions are downloaded eight at a time (the bucket was the slow step at 160 × 60 candidates); the rest of the pipeline is unchanged. The full build is reproducible from the committed `pack.json`, `overrides.json` and `wikipedia.lock.json` plus the builder's cache (API pages pinned to `inat_created_before`, Wikipedia pages by title); from a clean cache the iNaturalist side drifts only by deletions, and Wikipedia's plain-text extract API serves only the current revision, so the lock file cannot fetch an old one: the build warns for every article whose revision moved since the lock and re-pins it, which makes text drift a visible diff rather than a silent change.
- **Distribution**: the built pack is a GitHub Release asset (`pack-us-ca-la-v1`, `us-ca-la.zip`) pinned by SHA-256 in `packs/manifest.json`; `scripts/download-pack.sh` fetches and unpacks it and the Xcode Cloud post-clone hook runs it after the models. The repository was private then, so Xcode Cloud needed a `GITHUB_TOKEN` environment variable with contents read access on the workflow; it has been public since #13 and the download needs no credentials.
- **Result**: 160 species, 793 photos (five each but for White-throated Swift, Great Horned Owl and Common Yellowthroat with four and Yellow-chevroned Parakeet and American Barn Owl with three; no photo gap after the Bald Eagle override widened its candidate list to 200 and pinned the adults over the brown juveniles the score preferred), every species described, 147 with a size and 155 with a habitat line; 161 MB on disk (32 MB lens, 128 MB phone crops), a 165 MB zip. App-size check: the pack ships as the `BirdJournalKit_Pack.bundle` resource bundle, 161 MB in the built app against 11 MB for the ten-species pack (the debug simulator build is 589 MB in all, 77 MB of it the models), so the increase is 150 MB: under the 200 MB budget with the 1200 px phone crops kept. The first build took about 75 minutes (9,600 photo downloads); a rebuild from the cache takes about six. Originals smaller than the 552 × 368 lens crop are now skipped (five 300-pixel parakeet and gull photos had been upscaled into cards), and the phone crop never cuts tighter than the lens crop.
- Not in this issue: lookalikes (the table stays empty; no lens page shows them), a hand-written pass over the 160 derived texts beyond the override file, downloadable packs on the phone (#13).

## Album and credits on the phone (issue #11) — built 2026-09-27
- The phone's root list gets an **Album** row (sighting count) and a **Settings** screen; Settings holds the species pack screen and the **Credits** screen, so the credits are reachable from settings as the spec asks.
- Album: every `Sighting` newest first (`Sighting.newestFirst()`, a `FetchDescriptor` the `@Query` uses), one row each with the **2x center crop** of the stored frame (`FrameCrop` in the `Album` module: the middle half of each side, on whole pixels; the frame stays whole on disk), the species name, the date and the place. Tapping a row shows the crop across the screen and the record (names, when, where, match, glasses or phone). Species names come from the bundled pack by scientific name (the pack's `birdnet_label` column holds the scientific name, not the full label), else from the label's own common name, so an unpacked species is still named.
- Place: a sighting stores only a coordinate, so the album reverse-geocodes it with MapKit's `MKReverseGeocodingRequest` (iOS 26+; `CLGeocoder` is deprecated) once per run and coordinate, cached in `PlaceNames`, and shows "34.14° N, 118.29° W" until a name arrives or for good when offline. No location reads "No location".
- Frames are read as data, not by path: `URL.path()` percent-encodes the space in "Application Support", so `UIImage(contentsOfFile:)` on it finds nothing. The frame store is one per app (`BirdJournalApp.makeFrameStore()`), shared by the recorder and the screens through the `frameStore` environment value.
- Credits: "Powered by BirdNET" first, then both models (`ModelCredits` in `Identification`: BirdNET+ V3.0 preview 3.1, CC BY-SA 4.0, the Zenodo DOI; geomodel v3.0.4, Apache-2.0, the GitHub release) each linking its license deed and source, with the license and terms texts read from the bundled `Models` folder (`TERMS_OF_USE.txt` is Zenodo's terms naming CC BY-SA 4.0; `LICENSE-MODELS.md` and `ACCEPTABLE_USE.md` are the geomodel's; all pinned in `models/manifest.json`), then every photo in the bundled pack grouped by species with observer, license (linked to the Creative Commons deed, `PackPhoto.licenseURL`) and the observation link, and the pack's LICENSE text verbatim.
- Debug launch flags for simulator screenshots: `-inMemoryAlbum YES` keeps the album in memory and its frames in a fresh tmp folder; `-autoAlbum seed` seeds three sightings with generated frames and opens the album (`seed-detail` opens the newest); `-autoCredits YES` opens Settings then Credits. _Replaced in #28 by `-seedJournal YES` and `-autoScreen <name>`; see "The phone as a field journal"._
- Not in this issue: deleting sightings, a session record, sharing. Verified on the simulator: order, crop (the seed frame's border is cropped away), place names online, credits with all 50 photos.

## Pause, disconnect and error handling (issue #10) — built 2026-09-27, hardware run pending
- The listening run survives the glasses. Phase A showed a doff does not pause: the glasses end the session ("Session ended by device") and drop the link, and walking out of range looks the same. So when a session stops on the device side the run reads the glasses' state to tell the cases apart: `donState == .doffed` is a doff, a link that is not `.connected` is a lost link, and glasses still worn and connected for a 2 s grace period (a link drop can be reported a beat after the session ends) is the wearer quitting (two-finger or middle-finger tap), which ends the run as before.
- A doff or a lost link **pauses** the run instead of ending it: the lens releases Display and Inputs but keeps its pages, selection and saved marks (`GlassesLensSession.Phase.suspended`); the audio source drops the camera but keeps the engine's chunk stream open (the engine sees a silence, not an end, so the stack survives); the phone shows "Paused: glasses off" or "Paused: glasses disconnected" with Stop as the way out. The run then waits, as long as it takes, for the glasses to be connected and not doffed, starts a new session itself, puts the same pages back on the lens and continues the stream on it (three tries, 5 s apart; then the run ends failed and the Start button is the restart). Nothing restarts from the phone.
- A session the toolkit pauses (`.paused`, the touchpad; the mock's `captouch.tap()` does this) is shown on the phone as "Paused by the glasses" and resumes with the session; the lens re-sends the current card in case the display dropped it. Untested on hardware.
- A session that ends with a thermal, battery, peak-power, glasses-app-update or SDK-version error ends the run failed rather than waiting: the glasses will not restart it until the wearer does something.
- Problems the wearer should know about while the lens is reachable are a **problem page** (`LensPage.problem`) over whatever page they were on: "No location" when the first fix is denied or does not arrive (identification carries on without the regional filter, as decided in #6), and "Connection lost" as the first card after a dropped link comes back. Swipe right, Back or a tap is the way back to the covered page; the other swipes stay; species heard meanwhile append behind it. A doff and don shows no page: it was the wearer's own doing. The phone mirrors the card and shows the location state.
- Glasses that leave again during a resume are waited for again. Stop from the phone in any active phase (listening, deciding, paused, reconnecting) cancels the reconnection and tears down the lens, engine, stream and session the same way; every listener token lives in a bag that is emptied on every suspension and stop, so three start-stop cycles leave nothing behind (mock test; the run deallocates after).
- Verified on the mock: doff + power off then power on + don resumes the pages and the audio without a restart; power off while worn is a lost link and the return shows "Connection lost" with swipe right back; the touchpad pause and resume; Stop while paused; no location; three cycles. To verify on the glasses (DAT-SETUP-CHECKLIST.md): the real doff/don timings, walking out of range and back, the touchpad, and that a quit still reads "Ended by the glasses" after the grace period.

## Downloadable regional packs (issue #13) — built 2026-09-27
- **The index**: `index.json` on the rolling `packs` release of this repository (`PackIndex.defaultURL`), written by `scripts/publish-pack-index.sh` from `packs/manifest.json` (id, name, version, the zip's release URL, SHA-256, byte count), which now also carries each pack's `name` and a `bundled` flag: `scripts/download-pack.sh` fetches only the bundled packs into `packs/` (everything there ships in the app), `scripts/build-pack.sh` writes a non-bundled pack to `build/packs/<id>/`, and `scripts/pin-pack.sh` keeps the flag. The index lists the bundled pack too, so one file describes every pack; the phone shows it as "Included with the app" and never offers it.
- **The repository is public** (made so on 2026-09-27 for this issue: the phone fetches the index and the zips with no credentials, and GitHub serves private release assets only with a token, which an app must not ship). The project is MIT-licensed (`LICENSE` at the root; the models and packs carry their own), the photos are CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC with full attribution, so redistribution is lawful, and the DAT `ClientToken` and `MetaAppID` in `Info.plist` are attestation identifiers that ship inside every app's bundle, not secrets. Verified after the change: an anonymous fetch of the index and of the San Diego zip answers 200, and the app on the simulator downloads the pack from the real index with no override. `PackIndex.defaultURL` and the `repo` in `scripts/publish-pack-index.sh` and `scripts/download-pack.sh` are the three places that name the repository; the `-packIndexURL <url>` and `-autoDownloadPack <id>` debug flags point a simulator run at a local server (`python3 -m http.server` over a folder with `index.json` and the zips) when a pack is not published yet.
- **On the phone**: `PackLibrary` (in the `Pack` module, `@MainActor @Observable`) holds the bundled pack, every download under Application Support/Packs (excluded from backup: a pack is re-downloadable) and the index; the lens, the album and the credits look species and photos up across all packs, bundled first, so a species in two packs shows the bundled pack's card and a photo id resolves to whichever pack has it (two packs sharing a species share its iNaturalist photos). A download streams to tmp with progress, the SHA-256 is checked against the index, the zip is unpacked into `<id>.unpacking` by a small zip reader of our own (`ZipArchive`: stored and deflated entries as Python's `zipfile` writes them, CRC-checked, no zip64, entry names that escape the folder refused; Apple's Compression framework inflates, so no dependency), opened to prove it is a readable pack, then moved into place with `installed.json` recording the descriptor. A newer version in the index is offered as an update over an installed pack. Deleting removes the folder; the species are gone from the lens and the album at once (the album falls back to the label's common name).
- **Second pack**: `us-ca-sd`, San Diego County, the 40 most-observed species (place 829, queried 2026-09-27) as a starter: 40 species, 200 photos, 39 MB, release `pack-us-ca-sd-v1`. Widening it to the LA pack's 150-plus-heard-more-than-seen shape is content work for a later issue. The builder now retries dropped connections and 5xx answers (`http.py`); one `RemoteDisconnected` had ended a build.
- **Found on the way**: `URL.path()` percent-encodes the space in "Application Support" (the album had noted it); every path call in the pack code uses `path(percentEncoded: false)` and a test installs into a folder with a space. Verified on the simulator: the index fetched over HTTP, the San Diego pack downloaded with a progress bar, verified, unpacked and listed with its species; the install surviving a relaunch. Not verified on hardware: a species from a downloaded pack on the lens (the lookups are the same code path as the bundled pack's).

## Pack text that never ends mid-phrase, and the bundled pack's updates (issues #32 and #33) — built 2026-09-27
- **The rule** (#32): 58 of the 160 Los Angeles species had field marks cut mid-phrase with an ellipsis ("…buff or rust-colored streaks at…"), because the builder clipped the first plumage sentence at the 20-word budget. Now `take_sentences` takes whole sentences that fit, skips a sentence that does not for a later one that does, falls back to the first sentence's longest clause that fits (a comma, semicolon, colon, "and", "but", "while" or "with" boundary keeping at least half the budget, closed with a full stop), and otherwise returns nothing so the next source (the lead, then non-measurement sentences) gets its turn; a species with no fitting text has empty field marks and the lens shows its summary. Text never ends with an ellipsis; `SpeciesPackTests` checks the bundled pack for one.
- **The room**: the Display DSL has three text styles (heading, body, meta), so the details page sets the field marks in `meta` with the primary colour (`LensElement.passage`), and the field-marks budget doubles to 40 words (`FIELD_MARKS_WORDS`, `LensCardRenderer.detailsWordBudget` = 64 with the name, match rate, size and habitat, credit and button). The other pages keep the 40-word budget in body text. Checked on the mock lens: a 40-word details page fits the 600 px canvas.
- **Versions** (#33): every pack already carried a version through `pack.json`, the manifest, the index and the phone's `installed.json`, and downloads were offered an update; the bundled pack was never offered. Now the index's entry for the bundled pack at a newer version shows "Update to version N" on its row under "On this phone"; the download installs beside the others and `PackLibrary.packs` puts it in the bundled pack's slot (`bundledUpdate`), so the lens, the album and the credits read it instead; deleting the update (swipe on the row) returns to the copy in the app. A zip whose pack id differs from the index entry's is refused (`PackInstallError.wrongPack`). Text fixes to the bundled pack therefore ship by rebuilding, bumping `version` in `pack.json`, pinning, releasing and publishing the index, with no app update.
- **Packs**: `us-ca-la` and `us-ca-sd` rebuilt from the cache as version 2 with the new rule and released; the manifest and the index list them.

## Species list: calling now first, the highlight following the bird (issue #42) — built 2026-10-02, hardware check pending
- **The stack and the card pages never reorder**; "Stack never reorders" under "Lens UI" stands, since it protects the card the wearer is reading and the indices the pages, the saved marks and the selection use. The **list page gets a presentation order** (`SpeciesListOrder`, Identification) computed from the stack and a session clock on every update, with hysteresis so a chorus does not shuffle it. Raw recency re-sorted on every window is rejected: on a five-row lens page it jitters, and the glasses re-send from the top.
- **Calling now**: a species whose last window at or above the threshold (`Candidate.lastHeardAt`) is within the last **6 s** of session time (one 3 s window plus two 1.5 s hops, so one missed window does not drop the marker). Several species can be calling at once. The session clock is seconds since the run started listening (`ListeningSession.sessionTime`, the clock `CandidateRow` already read heard times against); the engine's window times run from the first chunk, a beat later, which the 6 s absorbs.
- **The order**: the calling section first (the newest caller, then the bird calling the longest, then the others newest first; "calling since" is when the species entered the section, not its last window, so two birds alternating keep their rows; with a third caller the previous newest drops to row three and row two stays put), then everything else most recently heard first, so a bird that goes quiet drops in at the top of that section and birds heard once sink on their own.
- **Marking**: a calling row on the lens has the speaker icon (`speakerWithThreeArcs`) before the name and the primary text colour; the selection keeps its caret and card background, so the two coincide. The photo page's strip gains "· calling now" for its own bird or "· Now: <name>" (the newest caller) when another is calling; the details page carries a strip with the same words only then, paid for out of the field marks' budget. On the phone a calling row reads "Calling now" in moss with a waveform glyph (static under Reduce Motion) and a moss ring on the thumbnail; the supporting text then ages "Heard just now" → "Heard N min ago" on a once-a-second tick while listening (it was computed only on render before).
- **Selection on the lens is species-keyed**: unpinned until the wearer swipes, the highlight sits on row one, so the calling bird is the highlighted row and a tap opens it. Swipe down or up pins it to a species (`LensStateMachine.pinnedSpecies`) and it stays on that bird as the list moves under it; a pinned bird pushed onto another page pages the view with it (the list pages by row position). **Swipe right on the list**, unused before, unpins and returns to the top. Opening a card leaves the pin as it was; paging to the next species on a card pins to that species, so coming back lands on it. Every other gesture is unchanged.
- **Turning the marker off**: a lapse changes nothing in the stack, so `GlassesLensSession` keeps the session clock's origin from the latest update and schedules one re-send at the lapse the order reports (one send per calling episode), and re-evaluates the order before the pages go back on the lens after a suspension. **Open risk, from the Toolkit facts**: a send may wake a dimmed display. To check on the glasses: let a bird's marker lapse with the display dimmed; if the re-send wakes it, fall back to clearing the marker on the next stack change only and record that here.
- **The phone** shows the same order with animated moves (none under Reduce Motion); the Review button can move under a thumb during a chorus, accepted for the glance use case. `ListeningCoordinator.orderedCandidates` is the order over the current stack; the remembered order is refreshed with the waveform (12 Hz, written only when it moves) and settled with nothing calling at Stop, so the review after a run is by recency.
- **Verified on the mock**: the list order, the marks and the strip, the pin surviving a card visit, and the lapse re-send (`LensMockSmokeTests`); the phone list with the example soundscape (`ListenUITests`). **Not verified on hardware**: the lapse re-send against the dimmed display (above), and the speaker icon's rendering on the real lens.

## A fresh location for every sighting, and the spot on a map (issue #44) — built 2026-10-02, hardware check pending

## Reference sounds in the packs (issue #41) — packs built 2026-10-06, phone playback and the lens 2026-10-07, hardware check pending
- **Reverses** the #28 line that kept reference audio out. All four phases of #41 are built: the builder's sounds stage, pack schema 3, the Swift `Pack` module's `PackSound`, the credits and the index's schema (2026-10-06), the phone's playback with scoring suppression and the lens play control (2026-10-07, below), and the other four packs rebuilt with sounds.
- **Licensing: option A.** xeno-canto first, ranked A/B grade first, then open licenses (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA) ahead of NonCommercial (CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, the posture the photos took with CC BY-NC), then grade. No-derivatives is refused: the clip is trimmed, filtered and re-encoded, which is an adaptation. ShareAlike clips keep their own license in the pack LICENSE. Audio licenses are stored with their version ("CC BY-SA 3.0"), since SA terms differ by version; photo licenses stay without one. xeno-canto's reply: they cannot grant permission (the recordists hold the rights; ask a recordist when a use is near a license's border), downloading is fine, but do not hit the per-second or per-hour rate limit or they block the client for a while.
- **Politeness is in the client, not in a person's care.** Every xeno-canto request, search or file, goes one at a time through one pacer at least 4 s apart, and a lock on the cache keeps a second build from requesting beside it (at most 900 an hour; third-party clients cite about 1,000 as the limit; the pacer refuses under 2 s); only the first page of up to 500 recordings is read; one search per species in the pack's country, a worldwide one only when the country has no usable song or call; at most three downloads per kind; a run stops at 1,500 requests; the first 429, 503 or 403 stops the build with no retry; a dropped connection or a 500/502/504 is tried at most three times, each try paced and counted, 10 s and then 20 s later; redirects are followed one paced, counted hop at a time; requests' errors, which carry the URL and so the key, are re-raised without it; every answer and file is cached, so a rerun resumes. `sounds.lock.json` (committed) pins every chosen recording with its credit, so a rebuild asks xeno-canto for nothing from a warm cache and only for the chosen files from a cold one. The key lives in `XENO_CANTO_API_KEY` in the developer's shell profile, is sent only as the API's `key` parameter, and the tests unset it.
- **Clip**: the loudest 8 s of the recording (after a 200 Hz high-pass), high-passed, loudness-normalized to −16 LUFS, 100 ms fades, mono 48 kHz AAC-LC at 64 kbps, about 65 KB. **Check**: BirdNET+ V3.0 in the app's 3 s windows 1.5 s apart; kept only when the species tops the mean scores (`accept` in the overrides names the other half of a split BirdNET+ carries both of: Hudsonian Whimbrel heard as *Numenius phaeopus*, American Yellow Warbler as *Setophaga petechia*). Rejected clips are listed in `report.json`. Untyped, juvenile, begging, subsong and playback-answering recordings are not candidates.
- **iNaturalist** research-grade observation sounds (open licenses only, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, as the issue's table asks) fill only a species xeno-canto left without any clip; they are untyped, so the `kind` column gains `sound` beside `song` and `call`. NPS recordings are not used (nothing needed them yet).
- **Schema 3**: the `sound` table (id `xc-<nr>` / `inat-<id>`, species_id, rank, kind, file, duration_ms, recordist, license, credit_line "Recordist, XC123, https://xeno-canto.org/123 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)", short_credit "Sound: Recordist, XC123", source_url, quality) and `sounds/`. The Swift reader opens schemas 2 and 3 (a pack downloaded before #41 still works, without sounds). `packs/manifest.json` and `index.json` carry `schema_version` / `schemaVersion` and the sound count; a descriptor of a newer schema shows "Needs a newer version of BirdJournal" and is not downloaded. App builds from before #41 do not read the field and fail a schema-3 download with "not a readable pack", leaving the installed pack in place.
- **Los Angeles v3**: 160 species, 794 photos and 266 clips (115 songs, 149 calls, 2 untyped iNaturalist sounds) for 153 species, 113 of them with both a song and a call; 92 clips are open-licensed (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA) and 174 CC BY-NC-SA. Seven species have no clip and are listed under `gaps.sounds`: Double-crested and Brandt's Cormorants, Turkey Vulture, American White Pelican, Muscovy Duck, Cinnamon Teal, Hooded Merganser (near-silent birds whose few recordings BirdNET hears as something else). The first build made 537 xeno-canto requests (about 3.4 per species) over about 80 minutes, paced by the photo work far below the cap; the two rebuilds after it (the split overrides, then the review fixes) made none and downloaded nothing, about five minutes each from the cache and the lock. ## Sounds page and viewfinder (2026-10-10, after the first field run with clips)
- **Silent glasses during the stream.** On the first field run a tap played nothing anywhere while the page read "Playing song…": iOS had routed the clip to the glasses' A2DP output (the route check passed), but the glasses did not play it while the camera streamed. The DAT docs say the in-stream microphone path leaves the glasses on A2DP and do not list this, so it is a hardware finding. **A clip now holds the stream**: `ReferenceSounds` asks the glasses run's `GlassesAudioSource` (`PlaybackStreamHold`) to take the camera off the session before the clip starts, and lets go when it ends; the camera goes back on the same session `restartDelay` (1 s) after the last clip, so a song then a call does not restart it in between. The session, the lens and the chunk stream stay up. The first hardware try never came back from a clip: the restart added a camera 1 s after the clip without waiting for the old one, whose `stop()` finishes asynchronously, and gave up after one failed attempt. The restart now waits for the old camera to report `.stopped` (up to 3 s) and the session to be `.started`, adds a fresh camera (a stopped one cannot be reused), and waits up to 8 s for its stream to reach `.streaming`; a start that does not get there is stopped and retried with another fresh camera, three attempts in all (the DAT docs report capabilities failing to start intermittently, with the next attempt succeeding), each logged under `glasses-audio`. A clip from the phone's profile during a glasses run holds it too. While held, nothing is heard, so the suppression has nothing to do until the restart; its 1.5 s tail still covers the first audio after.
- **Silence fills the pause.** The windower counts samples, so a gap would splice the audio either side into one window and leave the engine's clock behind the run's. The first audio after a hold is preceded by silence as long as the hold (up to 120 s). A doff's gap is not filled (unchanged).

  - While the viewfinder is open the stream runs at `.medium` (504×896) and 7 fps instead of `.low` at 2 fps, and the lens re-sends at most every 150 ms (was 0.5 s); the Bluetooth link to the Display may still set the pace. Whether the stream's field of view matches the photo's (9:16 stream, 3:4 photo) is to be checked on hardware, so the box may be approximate.

## A fast camera and listening that comes back (issue #46, 2026-10-25, after the fourth field run)

## The card: front and back, the flip, Heard or Seen, and rarity (issue #52) — built 2026-10-14
- **Rarity** (ADR-0006):
  - `RarityReader` (Identification) reads the geomodel's occurrence for the species at the sighting's coordinate and BirdNET week and labels it under `RarityCalibration`. No location, or a species the geomodel doesn't know (three pack species: Tyto furcata, Dryobates villosus, Dryobates borealis), gives no reading. Below the 0.03 floor is Exceptional and keeps its occurrence.
  - **Calibration 1** (`Sources/Identification/RarityCalibration.json`, written by `packbuilder calibrate-rarity --version 1`; never edited by hand): the geomodel over every species of the five pack definitions, on a 6 × 6 grid across each pack's bounding box, all 48 weeks, readings at or above the floor only (1.29 million). The bounds are the 50th, 20th and 5th percentiles: **Common from 0.763, Uncommon from 0.386, Rare from 0.101, Exceptional below**. So about half of what the packs expect is Common and one reading in twenty Exceptional. The pack species are mostly expected where their pack is (the median reading is 0.76), which is why the bands sit high. A new calibration takes the next version; saved cards keep theirs.
  - **At the add**: `SightingRecorder` saves the snapshot (occurrence, label, calibration version, week) with the sighting. The engine and the reader share one geomodel load (`BundledIdentification.geoModel`). A failed read records the sighting without rarity.
  - **Backfill**: sightings saved before rarity get one from their saved coordinate and date at the first launch, once per install (`rarityBackfilled` default; skipped for the debug in-memory album). The geomodel is read off the main actor; if a read fails, nothing is marked done and the next launch tries again. A saved snapshot is never read again, and a sighting without a location keeps none.
- **The card page** (`CardDetailView`, `Route.card`): the Cards view opens a card here; Sightings and Species still open the sighting's details, and the card links to them ("Sighting details": note editing, the map, removal). The labelled Flip control sits above the card, because a card is taller than the screen and the control must stay in reach. Share is in the toolbar as on the sighting.
  - **Front**: the Legendary label (editions wait for pack schema 4, so a repeat card has none yet), the art (a tap flips), the place, the species and scientific name, the rarity with its season ("Common here in mid October"), and the date and method. Captions, the type line and stats come with their tickets.
  - **Back** ("Encounter story", on the raised surface in the same frame): the date and local time, the place, Heard or Seen as a segmented control (saved at once, `JournalEdits.setMethod`), the note (edited in the sighting's details), the evidence ("Photo attached" with **View original photo** only when an original exists: an attached photo first, else the glasses photo), what the art is, and where the rarity came from.
  - **Original photo**: the glasses photo from the add counts, as CONTEXT.md defines it, so a glasses card reads "Photo attached" though the birder attached nothing by hand.
  - **Local time**: the sighting stores no time zone, so "local time" is the phone's current zone; a card read while travelling shows the time in that zone.
  - **Fallbacks**: "Place not recorded"; "Rarity unavailable for this place and season", with the reason on the back (no location saved, or no geomodel reading); missing art is a typographic face with the bird's name, labelled "No art yet"; the reference photo keeps its "Reference photo" label and on the back reads "not your bird" with its credit.
- **The flip** (`CardFlip`): both faces stay mounted in one stack, so the card keeps the taller face's footprint. An animated angle turns them about the vertical axis in 480 ms ease-in-out with slight perspective. Each face shows only on its half of the turn and is counter-rotated, so text is never mirrored. Only the face that is up is in the accessibility tree and takes touches. That follows the state rather than the turn, so mid-turn VoiceOver already has the new face. After a flip VoiceOver's focus moves to the Flip control, since the art that may have been activated is now on the hidden face. The control's label changes ("Flip to encounter story" / "Flip to artwork"), its value reads Front or Back, and a flip announces "Back, encounter story" or "Front, collectible art", with a light haptic. Which face is up comes from the state, not the animation, so rapid taps retarget the turn and settle on one face. Reduce Motion switches faces with no turn and no animation. Child controls on the back act alone: View original photo opens a sheet and the card stays on its back.
- **Debug flags**: `-autoScreen card|card-back` (the newest card) and `-reduceMotion YES` (the flip as with the system setting, which a UI test can't turn on). The seeds carry their real geomodel rarity, and a sixth seed (a Common Blackbird with no place, no photo and no pack) shows every fallback. `scripts/screenshots.sh` captures both faces.
- **Tests**: Identification `RarityTests` (the real geomodel at fixed Los Angeles weeks, one species per band, below the floor, no location, unknown species); pack builder `test_rarity.py`; app `SightingRecorderTests` (snapshot at the add, none without a location, backfill once, never recomputed), a coordinator test with a scripted location, `CardTextTests`, `CardFlipTests` and the method in `JournalEditsTests`; `CardFlipAccessibilityTests` hosts the card in a window and walks the UIAccessibility tree VoiceOver reads (one face, at rest and mid-turn); UI `CardFlipUITests` (art and Flip control with the Front/Back value, rapid taps (XCUITest waits for the app to settle between taps, so a tap landing mid-turn is covered by the design, the face following the state, rather than by the test), Reduce Motion, View original photo not flipping, Seen persisting, the fallbacks and the reference-photo label).

## Pack art (issue #57) — built 2026-10-20 to 2026-11-01; Los Angeles v7 reviewed and published
- **The step** (`packbuilder/src/packbuilder/pack_art.py`, run by `packbuilder build` after the photos are chosen): one painting per species per pack on comfybox's FLUX.3 ComfyUI over the tailnet (the link ran at about 2.5 MB/s, against 420 KB/s during #49, so the build paints from the Mac), with the committed `pack-art` workflow in the set's edition theme (`set.edition.theme`, prompts v8 and on). The bird is isolated on white and every painting goes through the #49 fidelity gate (`painting.py`, shared with the bake-off). A species with no open-license photo is painted from text (`pack-art-text`, the only template that names the species); it must be reviewed by hand. A Bushtit from text came out a generic warbler, so expect to reject such paintings or list them as gaps.
- **The reference** is the species' best-lit photo among the pack's chosen photos (`lighting`: the 75th percentile of L* over the middle of the bird's box, less for a bird darker than the frame around it and for blown highlights), or the one `overrides.json` pins as `art.reference`. The lens score's first photo was often a flight shot or a dim bird. The lighting score helps, but it can't tell a flight shot, a flock or a head-only close-up from a perched bird, and it favours a slim bird against bright sky. In the Los Angeles first pass, 37 species of 160 needed a pin (flight or hovering 8, several birds 3, head-only close-ups 2, dim, backlit or distant 8, half-hidden or odd poses 10, and a dull female where the male is the bird birders picture 6), each with its reason in `overrides.json`. Follow-up for the other packs: score only the bird's own pixels (a variant measured against the background caught backlit birds but tied most others at full marks), and reject a reference whose cut-out holds more than one bird.
- **The crop** keeps the whole bird: a 3:4 box inside a landscape photo cut a House Finch's head off and FLUX.3 invented one, so the reference crop now reaches past the photo's edge, filled with its own colours blurred, which the isolation paints white.
- **Prompts v9**: v8's "it perches naturally" put the coot, the gull and the Mallard on citrus branches; v9 says it "perches, stands or swims ... where a bird like it lives: on a branch, a rock, the ground, the shore or the water". That in turn stands most birds on a rock by a stream. Two other wordings were tried on six species and were worse: "as it would be in life ... on something that suits it" stood every bird on a bare mound (the Mallard on a dry hill), and naming kinds of bird ("a songbird on a twig, a waterbird at the water's edge") painted extra birds into the scene. The look across the set is the maintainer's call on the sheets.
- **Prompts v10** (the maintainer's review of the v9 sheets, 2026-11-01): most paintings had the wrong scale or an odd composite. A House Finch on a twig in its reference stood on a rock as big as itself, and a Mourning Dove whose wire the isolation removed floated by a stray twig. So the setting comes from the reference, not the model: each species has `art.setting` in `overrides.json`, one of perch, trunk, flight, ground, rock, shore, wade or water, classified by hand from its reference on the sheets (Los Angeles: 80 perch, 28 ground, 18 shore, 15 water, 7 rock, 7 wade, 3 trunk, 2 flight). Any perch off the ground (a branch, wire, fence, post or rail) is painted as a branch, the maintainer's call. A woodpecker clinging to a trunk keeps a trunk, and the two flight shots (Osprey, White-throated Swift) fly above a branch. The bird stays about half the card's height, close up, with its setting at its own scale and the edition theme far behind. Its length (the middle of the description's range, or `art.length_cm` for the 13 without one) sizes the branch (pencil-thin under 20 cm, finger-thick to 35 cm, wrist-thick above) and how long a leaf is beside it. Without a size the 52 cm Red-tailed Hawk perched on a flimsy flowering twig. Trials on 23 species at v9's seeds are in `build/art-trial/` (not committed). Padding the isolated bird onto a larger canvas to make it smaller changed nothing: FLUX.3 sizes the bird from the prompt, and the Mourning Dove, whose reference crop fills its frame, still paints large. The setting is in the lock, so changing one species' setting repaints only that species.
- **The lock** (`packbuilder/packs/<id>/art.lock.json`, committed) pins per species the source photo, workflow, isolation and prompts versions, the theme, the seeds, the painting's SHA-256 (its name in `cache/art/paintings/`), its fidelity and the review. A warm lock paints nothing; changed inputs repaint and go back to review; a painting gone from the cache is repainted from its locked seeds. Isolated birds are cached by photo, seed, workflow and the isolation prompt's own hash, so a pack-art prompt bump reuses them.
- **The review**: contact sheets in `build/pack-art/<id>/` (each reference beside its painting with its state and fidelity, and an overview) and `packbuilder art-review` (approve, reject with a reason, gap). Only approved paintings ship. `--approve-all` skips paintings from text and the ones the gate flagged, which need an approve by name. A rejected painting is repainted by the next build with the next round of seeds; a gap gets none and is listed under `gaps.art` in `report.json`, and its cards show another pack's painting of the species, else the reference photo. Paintings not yet approved are listed under `unreviewed.art`, and a pack with any is not ready to publish (`PackArtTests` fails on one).
- **The format**: 864 × 1152 AVIF at quality 60 (`writer.py`), about 120 KB, half a JPEG's size at the same look. The v5 pack was 174 MB on disk against the 200 MB budget, and 160 JPEG paintings at quality 85 (about 250 KB each) would have passed it. iOS decodes AVIF natively. `pack_art` rows carry the file, the source photo ids (JSON) and "pack-art@3 bird-isolate@1 prompts@9". The LICENSE lists each painting with the photo it was painted from and its photographer's credit, and states that paintings from CC BY-SA photos are shared under CC BY-SA.
- **Phone**: `SpeciesPack` reads `pack_art`; `PackLibrary.packArt` takes the art of the pack whose set the card is in, then any other pack's. ## The Mock Device Kit pipeline: the hang, timeouts and the Chrome lens (issue #82) — built 2026-11-01
- **Root cause (toolkit bug, worked around).** The mock suites hung (#51, #54, #55, twice on 2026-11-01) because the toolkit's mock link wedges: when one mock device is torn down and the next paired while the old link's stream I/O is still closing, one `MWDATCore` run-loop thread (a single one for the whole process) spins at 100% CPU inside one block calling `CFReadStreamRead` on a CFStream bound pair (`sample` of the host, reproduced on BJ-82). From then on nothing in that test host gets an answer over the mock link. So every following lens test fails with "No connected display glasses." (the device selector never sees the new device), a permission check awaits forever, and the host does not exit after the run. The DAT reference says it: the async `disable()` returns while "the underlying stream I/O may still be closing on its background thread". The CoreBluetooth "XPC connection invalid" lines are noise: passing runs log them on every `enable()`. Load and parallel xcodebuilds make it likelier but are not the cause: run side by side under the same load, on two simulators, the old teardown wedged and the new one did not.
- **Repro**: `MockDeviceKitStressProbe` cycles pair, a started session with a camera stream, and teardown 40 times in one host (`TEST_RUNNER_BIRDJOURNAL_MDK_STRESS=40`, `TEST_RUNNER_BIRDJOURNAL_MDK_TEARDOWN=bare|unpair|settle|settled`; the command, with the longer allowance it needs, is in the probe's header). The old teardown (`enable` … `disable`) wedged at cycle 7; an awaited `unpairDevice` alone failed at cycle 2; the settle alone and the full teardown each passed 40 of 40. Not reported upstream yet: a repro outside this app would be the next step.
- **Workaround** (`MockDeviceKitTests.withMockDisplay`): wait until the paired device itself (`glasses.deviceIdentifier`) is connected, not any device (a stale one satisfied the old wait); tear down with an awaited `unpairDevice`, an awaited `disable()`, then a wait for `Wearables.devices` to empty and 300 ms more (DEBUG `GlassesConnection.tearDownMockDeviceKit`, which the DEBUG `unpairMockGlasses()` uses too). The interruption tests stop their run before the teardown (`withRun`), not in `defer { Task { … } }`, which ran the stop after `disable()` had begun. Test code and DEBUG code only; the glasses path in production is unchanged.
- **Timeouts** (a hang is a failure, never a stall):
  - The root `MockDeviceKitTests` suite has `.timeLimit(.minutes(2))`, which applies to every nested test (the longest takes about 25 s). Checked with `--hang-probe`: a test awaiting a continuation nobody resumes, or one spinning a thread, records "Time limit was exceeded" at the limit. Since it never returns, xcodebuild then ends the host ("Restarting after unexpected exit, crash, or test timeout"), the run fails (exit 65) and the script exits 1.

## Circles: create, invite by link or QR, join, leave (issue #61) — built 2026-11-01, deploy, DNS and device check pending

## Lore and captions (issue #56) — built 2026-11-01; Los Angeles lore approved and published in v8
- **The fact sheet** (`packbuilder/factsheet.py`, the `fact` table): up to eight sentences of the species' Wikipedia article at the pinned revision (the description's source, so a fully hand-written description takes no article facts), then its stats told as short sentences, each credited to its dataset ("It nests in a half cup or a cup." from BIRDBASE; the IUCN category as "The IUCN Red List rates it Least Concern."). The spec's "stats plus up to about eight short facts" read as eight from the article on top of the stats, about 15 a species. The article's come first because they are the richer: AVONET's "rock" habitat and BIRDBASE's "cup" are coarse beside "always near water" and "cemented in its place with mud". The sentences are picked by rule: the article's first (it names the bird), then sentences about how the bird lives (feeding, nesting, voice, movement), then where it lives, then neutral ones (a range, a date), then naming and classification; within each tier the sections are taken in turn (split at every heading, so Breeding under Behaviour counts as breeding; the lead, behaviour and diet, breeding, voice, people, names, range, status, appearance), at most two from one kind besides the lead, and a sentence that mostly repeats one taken is left out. A sentence fits when it stands alone (6 to 32 words, whole, no measurements, not opening on "This", "However", "Both" and the like). Facts are ids `f1`, `f2`, ... in sheet order. The first rule (one fitting sentence of each kind in turn, classification included) gave the Black Phoebe "the largest family of birds on earth" and three range sentences, and none of "always near water", sallying from a perch or the mud nest the lead holds; the maintainer's review of the trial asked for those, and the tiers find them.

## Finds in the circle feed: automatic sharing, private, a secret spot, first for the circle (issue #62) — built 2026-11-01, deploy, thumbnail upload and the two-device check pending
- **The find policy** (`Share/FindPolicy.swift`, pure, beside the share policy): an add publishes only when signed in and sharing (`publishesAutomatically`); private gives nothing. The payload is the species (BirdNET label, scientific and common names), Heard or Seen, the place, the time to the minute in UTC (`2026-11-01T08:15:00Z`, seconds dropped), the Legendary and rarity flags, `threatened`, and the art reference (a pack-art thumbnail's key). Never a coordinate, the note, the caption or a frame: the encoded JSON has exactly those ten keys (tested). The place is the city `PlaceNames` gives, else none; a label that looks like a coordinate (the album's fallback) is none. **A threatened species, or one no pack rates** (unknown counts as threatened, as for captions, #56), is always at "a secret spot". The Legendary flag is the add's and frozen there: Legendary passing to another card later publishes nothing (spec). Extras of a card shared by hand are #66's.
- **"Share my finds automatically"** (Settings → Account, under the account row; `shareFindsAutomatically` in UserDefaults, on when unset). Off is private: later adds stay on the phone, and turning it back on doesn't publish them. Finds already shared stay until the birder removes the sighting (my choice: the spec only says off is private; deleting everything on a switch felt too destructive to do silently). A published find's Heard-to-Seen still updates while private, as a correction.
- **The outbox** (`Circles/FindOutbox.swift`, `@MainActor`): one entry per sighting, the latest change winning (a put then a delete before either went is a delete; a removal undone is a put), persisted as JSON in Application Support (`FindOutbox.json`; the debug in-memory album keeps it in tmp). It remembers which sightings each account published (kept after a delete, so an Undo after the delete went puts the find back), so Heard or Seen (`JournalEdits.setMethod`) puts again, a removal (the journal's, at once rather than when the undo window closes, so a quit in the window can't leave the find behind; and undoing an add in a run) deletes, and Undo puts back. **Every entry belongs to the account (`sub`, now read from the ID token) that published it**: signed out nothing goes, and signing in as another account never sends the first one's; signing back in as the same one sends what waited. The payload is built when the entry is sent, through the policy, from the sighting as it is then, so an offline add carries the city once the phone can name it; a sighting gone by then drops its put. It sends after each change, on sign-in, when `NWPathMonitor` says the network is back, when the app becomes active, and on its own retry. Failures back off 2, 4, 8 … seconds up to five minutes; refusals a retry can't fix (422, 404, other 4xx but 408 and 429) drop the entry with a log line. One drain at a time; a caller during one waits for it.
- **On the server** (`backend/src/birdjournal_api/finds.py`, `finds_api.py`): `PUT /v1/finds/{sighting id}` (a UUID, stored lower-case; 201 new, 200 updated), `DELETE /v1/finds/{sighting id}` (204, also when there's nothing to delete), `GET /v1/circles/{id}/feed?limit=1..50 (default 20)&cursor=` (current members only, else 404; `next_cursor` opaque). Items in the single table, beside the circles':
  - `ACCOUNT#<sub>` / `FIND#<sighting id>`: the find, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `circle_ids`, the circles it went to (finds by owner; keyed by owner so two accounts' ids never meet).
  - `CIRCLE#<id>#FEED` / `<found at minute>#<sub>#<sighting id>`: a feed item per circle, the find's copy with its owner, read newest first. **Partitions of their own**, not under `CIRCLE#<id>`, so #61's meta-and-members queries never page through finds.
  - `CIRCLE#<id>#SPECIES` / `<scientific name>#<found at>#<sub>#<sighting id>`: the same items by species then time.
  - `FIND#<sub>#<sighting id>` / `REACTION#…`: **reactions go here (#63)**; a delete removes the whole partition.
  
  **No index** (no GSI): every access pattern is a key or a prefix. Fan-out reads the owner's current circles (`ACCOUNT#<sub>` / `CIRCLE#…`) on the **first** put only; a repeated put updates the circles recorded then and no others, so joining never backfills, not even through an update, and leaving keeps the items (a later delete still takes them out of a circle the owner left). A put that moves the time or species deletes the old items first. Not one transaction (an account can be in more circles than a transaction holds): a put writes the find, with its circles, before its feed items, and a delete removes the items before the find, so the find always names every circle that may hold an item of it; a half-done put or delete is finished by the outbox's retry (into the circles first named, even one left since), and every write is idempotent. The feed's owner names are read with the page, so a rename shows at once.
- **What the server refuses to hold**: any field beyond the policy's (`extra="forbid"`: `latitude`, `location`, `coordinate`, `note` are 422 and nothing is written); a place with a decimal number of two or more decimals, a degree sign or minutes after a number (`34 8' N`); over 80 characters or a control character. `threatened: true` stores "a secret spot" whatever place came. Art keys must be the art CDN's, of their kind (`packs/…` for `pack`, `art/…` for `painted`), `.jpg`/`.png`/`.webp`, no `..`. A time without a zone is 422; seconds are dropped.
- **First for the circle** is read with the feed (spec: "computed at read time"): for each species on the page, one query of `CIRCLE#<id>#SPECIES` with `begins_with(<scientific name>#)`, `Limit 1`, oldest first; the earliest surviving item is first, ties broken by owner and sighting id. ## The tip jar and listening test flakes (issue #93) — built 2026-11-01

- **The tip jar's "timeout" was a sandbox sign-in, not load.** A simulator's StoreKit uses an `SKTestSession`'s configuration (`BirdJournal.storekit`) only in processes launched after the first session registered it (in StoreKit's `group.com.apple.storekit` container, `…/Octane/com.matthewcelia.mybirdjournal/Configuration.storekit`). So the first purchase in a new simulator's first test host goes to the sandbox and waits on "Sign in to Apple Account" until xcodebuild's 180 s allowance; the restarted host then passes, which made it look like a load flake. Every field failure (#88, #57, #61) was the first run with the tip jar on a new `BJ-<n>` simulator; on an erased simulator it hung 4 of 4 times, alone, at load 18. Waiting in the same host (15 s) does not help, nor does a per-test session reset or not, nor a StoreKit configuration on the scheme's Test action (xcodebuild ignores it).
- **The fix is a primer, not a change to the tip jar**: `scripts/build-and-test.sh` checks the simulator for the registered configuration (`storekit_config_registered` in `scripts/lib/simulator.sh`) and, when it is missing, runs `TipJarTests/loadsThreeTips()` once as the `storekit-prime` run before the app runs (one more test host launch, about 15 s, once per simulator; every later run skips it). A failed primer is a warning, not a failed run: the runs below still go ahead. If StoreKit ever moves that file, the check says no every time and each run just primes again. Running the tip jar tests from Xcode on a brand-new simulator can still hang once; run anything that creates the session first, or use the script.
- **TipJarTests wait on nothing timed**: the Ask to Buy test awaits the outcome through `Observations` instead of polling 50 × 100 ms, and the suite has a one-minute time limit so any future wait that never ends fails in a minute, not three. With the hang gone, the first load loop (load 570–860) showed two races #59's checks had hidden: once `Transaction.unfinished` still listed the tip `TipJar` had just awaited `finish()` on, and once `session.allTransactions()` held one tip after two purchases. StoreKit's daemon and the session's record catch up a moment after the calls return, and neither has an event to await (`SKTestTransaction` has no finished state), so those reads go through `settles`, which re-reads until they hold with no deadline of its own: a tip that is never finished still fails, at the suite's time limit, naming what never settled. The shared session and its per-test reset stay as #59 left them (the experiments ruled them out).
- **The listening "window count" flake was an ordering race, not a clock**: the engine yields a window's `.window` report before the `.stack` it changed, and `playbackIsNotHeard` read the stack as soon as `windowsScored` reached 3, sometimes between the two (it saw 2). The count itself already runs on the fed audio's sample time (`ManualAudioSource` and the engine's window starts), so no clock seam was needed: the test now waits for the stack it asserts on, and checks the final stack after Stop, which drains the engine. `waitUntil` has no 5 s wall-clock deadline any more; the suite's one-minute time limit ends a wait that never comes true, and the cancellation names the wait.

## Pushes: notable finds now, the rest in a digest (issue #63) — built 2026-11-01, deploy, the APNs key and the two-device check pending
- **The decision** (`backend/src/birdjournal_api/pushes.py`): only a **new** find pushes (a repeated put sends nothing, as the spec says). Its recipients are the current members of the circles it went to, minus the owner (never pushed about their own finds), anyone who muted the owner, anyone for whom every one of those circles is muted, and anyone with no registered phone. A member in two of the circles hears once. **Notable** (Legendary, Rare or Exceptional, or the circle's first of the species in a circle the recipient hasn't muted) pushes at once to each of the recipient's phones: an alert (title "Maya Ortiz found a Black Phoebe", body the reasons and the place, "Legendary · First for Sunday Group · Pasadena"; a secret spot reads "At a secret spot") plus `content-available`, with `find`, the summary the lens banner (#67) reads: owner and name, sighting, species, method, place, minute, Legendary, rarity, `circle_first`, and `circle_ids`, only the recipient's own circles the find reached (a tap opens the first; other members' circles stay theirs). Circle first at write time is the feed's own query (`FindStore.is_circle_first`) run after the items are written, so an offline find published late from earlier in the day takes the first and pushes. Goal completion (also notable in the spec) arrived with #65 (below).
- **The digest**: any other find goes into `DIGEST` / `<recipient>#<friend>` (string sets of the sighting ids and of the circles they came through, and `first_at`), and the recipient's phones get a **silent push** (`content-available` only, `apns-push-type: background`, priority 5) so the feed refreshes. Every 15 minutes the flush sends each buffer whose recipient's last alert about that friend (`ACCOUNT#<recipient>` / `PUSHED#<friend>`, written by notable and digest pushes alike, not by silent ones) is two hours old or more, or never was. So a friend's first ordinary find of the day arrives within 15 minutes and later ones at most every two hours. The flush reads the finds back, so a find deleted meanwhile is left out and a buffer whose finds are all gone sends nothing; it drops the buffer, unsent, when by then the friend is muted, or every circle the finds came through is muted or left, or the recipient has no phone. ## Goals: the automatic monthly goal and custom goals (issue #65) — built 2026-11-01, deploy and the two-device check pending
- **The monthly goal** (`backend/src/birdjournal_api/goals.py`): one per circle per calendar month **in the circle's time zone** (the creator's device's, #61). Target: the larger of 5 and 115 % of last month's distinct species, **rounded half up in integer hundredths** (`(n × 115 + 50) // 100`: 10 gives 12, 20 gives exactly 23, where floating point gives 22.999…), or **10 in the month the circle was made** (the calendar month, so a circle made on the 31st has 10 for a day and its second month usually the floor of 5, as the spec words it). The target is fixed when the goal is made; deletes in last month don't move it. Its title is "<Month> goal"; it can't be deleted (403 `automatic_goal`).
- **The rollover** is a second EventBridge rule beside the digest flush (#63), `cron(0/15 * * * ? *)`, invoking the API's function with `{"birdjournal_job": "roll_over_goals"}` (no retries; `infra/birdjournal_infra/goals.py`). On the quarter hours, not hourly, because every UTC offset is a whole, half or (Nepal, Chatham) three-quarter hour, so each zone's midnight is caught at once. It reads one partition, `GOAL_ROLLOVER` / `<circle id>` (time zone, the last month it made), and touches only circles whose month has turned; an empty circle is passed over. A goal that has **ended** completes no more: a late find shared by hand doesn't announce last March's goal. "Once" is at most once: completion is noted before delivery, so a failed or fully muted delivery isn't retried.
